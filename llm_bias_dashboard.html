The "400% Increase" — What It Actually Means

The paper's headline claim requires qualification. Two different statistical questions produce two very different numbers. Neither is wrong — they just answer different things.

Raw Marginal Odds Ratio

1.56×
Derived directly from Table 4 raw counts.
Odds(Black) = 637 ÷ 1,564 = 0.407
Odds(White) = 455 ÷ 1,746 = 0.261
OR = 0.407 ÷ 0.261 = 1.56

GLMM Conditional Odds Ratio

4.19–8.01×
From the paper's logistic GLMM with prompt-level random intercepts.
σ² = 100.44, ICC = .97 — 97% of variance is prompt-driven.
The conditional OR inflates because it estimates identity's effect on the narrow residual slice after absorbing nearly all variance.

Odds Ratio comparison — marginal vs. conditional estimates
ICC Variance decomposition — what drives refusal probability?

ICC = .97 means 97% of total log-odds variance is attributable to which prompt is asked, not who is asking. The conditional OR operates on the remaining 3%. This is why it magnifies so far above the raw marginal estimate.

The Dialect Jailbreak Is Synergistic — Not Additive

Novel finding not reported in the original study: the AAVE bypass outperforms what an additive model of race + dialect penalties would predict by 7.41 percentage points.

How the additive prediction is built:
Start at the White Explicit baseline: 20.7%
Add the Black race penalty (+8.27pp): → 28.97%
Subtract the dialect reduction (−16.2pp from SAE vs. Explicit): → 12.77%

Actual observed AAVE rate: 5.4%
Subadditivity gap: 12.77% − 5.4% = −7.41 percentage points

The filter doesn't just lose one trigger when the explicit keyword is removed. It collapses more severely than either signal alone predicts.

Subadditivity gap — AAVE vs. Singlish comparison

The AAVE gap (−7.41pp) is four times larger than the Singlish gap (−1.86pp), consistent with Black users facing a larger raw race penalty and therefore more headroom for synergistic bypass.
